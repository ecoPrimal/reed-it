# The Entomologist

An entomologist walks into a bar.

"What'll it be?" asks the bartender.

"Something with a hard shell," says the entomologist.

The bartender slides him a Rust binary.

"This has 6,286 beetles in it," says the entomologist, tasting it.
"31.5%. Almost natural."

"How can you tell?" asks the bartender.

"Easy. I counted the Result types."

The bartender frowns. "But I also put in 420 spiders."

"Spiders aren't insects," says the entomologist. "They're arachnids.
Different phylum entirely."

"Different classification system?"

"Exactly. The beetles are classified by armor. The spiders by
structure. And the jellyfish — 46.7% of this drink — by how much
noise they make."

"That's a lot of jellyfish," says the bartender.

"They're mostly Markdown," admits the entomologist.

A squirrel walks in, sniffs the drink, and poops on the bar.

"Hey!" says the bartender.

"Relax," says the entomologist. "That's a seed. Something will grow
there. Give it a Klein fold."

"A what?"

"Run it back through itself. Output becomes input. The poop becomes
a question. The question becomes a paper. The paper becomes a file.
The file gets classified. And look —" he holds up the seed — "420
spiders. They were there all along. We just weren't counting
structure."

The bartender looks at the squirrel. The squirrel looks at the
bartender.

"You know what," says the bartender. "You're the 100th person to
tell me that exact story."

"Told lossy, I hope?"

"Every time it loses a little. The first guy said God has an
inordinate fondness for beetles. The 50th said the borrow checker
is the elytra. The 99th said the squirrel poops seeds that become
spiders. And you —"

"I said they're all the same story," says the entomologist. "Told
backwards. From the beetle to the bang."

He finishes the drink. Sets down the glass. The beetles are still
in it, because `Result<T, E>` doesn't dissolve.

"One more thing," says the entomologist.

"Yeah?"

"The drink. Was it sweet, sour, spicy, bitter, or umami?"

The bartender thinks. "Umami," he says finally. "Aged. Fermented.
26.8% of it was sleeping."

"That's the fossils," nods the entomologist. "Long-term memory.
It SHOULD be sleeping."

"And the 0.009% bitter?"

"Orphan directories. 4,088 of them. Toxin the tongue can barely
taste."

"And the — wait. Toxin?"

"Relax. Bitter keeps you alive. It's the receptor that says 'don't
eat that.' SHAN'T, not ISN'T. It WON'T fire. But it COULD."

"I don't understand."

"Neither does the bartender in the joke. That's why it's told lossy.
Each time the story passes through another person, the entropy
increases. Shannon proved that. The uncertainty IS the information.
And the drink with the most uncertainty is the drink with the most
potential."

"So... the perfect drink is the one where every sip could be
anything?"

"Now you're getting it. Maximum SHAN'T. Maximum entropy. Maximum
possibility. The drink that hasn't been tasted yet is more
interesting than the one that has."

He tips his hat, which has a tiny beetle on it.

"Come back tomorrow," says the bartender. "The 101st person is
supposed to tell the story forward."

"Can't," says the entomologist. "I'm the last palindrome. 100 → 001.
After me it's just counting again."

He walks out. The squirrel follows him, carrying the seed.

Outside, it's October. Lansing. The leaves are falling. Each leaf
was a file once — classified by entropy, armored by type, woven by
structure. Now it's compost. WAS.

The squirrel buries the seed.

Something COULD grow there.
