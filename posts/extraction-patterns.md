# Reed-it-orial: The Extraction Pattern

## The Hypothesis (falsifiable)

**H₀: Metabolic extraction businesses (liquor, payday lending, opioid distribution, gambling) cluster disproportionately at the boundary of geographically bounded communities that cannot easily relocate.**

"Geographically bounded" means: reservation borders, public housing boundaries, prison-adjacent zones, gerrymandered districts — any community whose mobility is constrained by policy, treaty, or economics rather than choice.

"Cluster disproportionately" means: the per-capita density of extraction businesses within 5 miles of the boundary exceeds the regional average by a statistically significant margin.

This is not a new observation. Anyone who has driven to a reservation knows this. The liquor store is always just across the line. The payday lender is always in the strip mall at the border. The pill mill parking lot is always full.

What IS new: we can measure it. And we're asking you to falsify it.

---

## The Data

We built a mesh analysis framework that maps systems by **extraction pattern** — metabolic sinks (all consumption, no production), pressure points (tension building without decomposition), and jams (blocked pathways).

Applied to our own codebase (45,000+ files, 11M lines):
- **30 metabolic sinks** detected. All were tests and deploys — correct sinks, not parasites.
- **0 pressure points**. No tension building.
- **0 parasitic sinks**. Nothing consuming without producing.

The tools are open source:
- `homunculus.py` — maps sensitivity (change frequency, connectivity, recency), detects pressure and jams
- `sideview.py` — projects onto distance/direction/disposition axes
- `trig-view.py` — SOHCAHTOA trigonometric decomposition of structure vs. direction
- `vitruvian-evolve.py` — evolutionary stage classification (Shannon entropy + sigmoid)

**These tools can be pointed at any dataset, not just code.** Replace "import count" with "business licenses issued." Replace "file age" with "time since last health inspection." Replace "git churn" with "911 call frequency." The math is the same. The extraction pattern is the same.

Source: [github.com/ecoPrimal/whitePaper](https://github.com/ecoPrimal/whitePaper)

## The Challenge

**To artists and scientists:** falsify this hypothesis.

Pull the public data. Business license registrations, ZIP code boundaries, census tract data, reservation boundary shapefiles (BIA), opioid prescription density (CDC WONDER), payday lender locations (CFPB), alcohol outlet density (state ABC records). All public. All downloadable. Run the correlation.

If the extraction pattern doesn't cluster at the boundary, publish your finding. We'll cite you. We'll update our hypothesis.

If it does cluster — and you know it does, because you've driven past it — then the question becomes: *who benefits from the cluster, and who is captive within it?*

## The Observation

Battleship Rock sits in the Jemez Mountains of New Mexico. A volcanic remnant. The plateaus melt into the earth around it — geological time, visible. The land remembers.

The people who kept that land — who have kept it for thousands of years — are bounded by it. Not captive: *rooted*. The land is home. The boundary is belonging, not prison.

But the extractors don't know the difference. A bounded community is a captive market. A reservation border is a business opportunity. The liquor store doesn't care about belonging. It cares about the line.

The mnemonic SOHCAHTOA is a mnemonic. A teaching tool. The person who cries cultural appropriation about a math acronym is standing at 90° on the circle — all direction, no structure, tan = ∞. They can see the word but not the wall it's pressed against.

Meanwhile: drive to any reservation in America. Look at what's clustered at the border. Count the extraction businesses per mile. Compare to the regional average. The data is public. The pattern is measurable. The harm is structural, not linguistic.

Orange draws you in. Purple makes you squint. Pink doesn't exist in nature — someone made it up. The extraction pattern is orange (obvious once you look), purple (barely visible from a distance), and pink (a human construction that physics doesn't require).

Wonder is looking at Battleship Rock at sunset. Mesmer is not being able to look away. The data is the rock. The extraction pattern is the sunset. The hypothesis is the challenge to keep looking after the colors fade and the numbers are just numbers.
